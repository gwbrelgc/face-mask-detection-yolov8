1. Description

Students must choose a practical Computer Vision (Image-based) problem from datasets available on Kaggle or a similar platform. The possible tasks include classification, object detection, segmentation,. The implementation must use Deep Learning techniques covered during the course, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Object Detection and Segmentation (YOLO, U-Net). At the end, each group must present the problem, their solution, and a critical analysis.

It is recommended to use Python and the libraries covered in class. The topic must be chosen by October 30, 2026, and discussed with the professor for validation. Some suggested examples include:

    Detection/Classification of Sign Language Gestures

    Detection/Identification of Faces or Voices (Spectrograms)

    Emotion Recognition in Faces and Speech

    Object Identification in Everyday Scenes

    Image Sequence Processing (Videos or Cameras)

    Identification of License Plates, Traffic Signs, or Anomalous Objects in Highways, Airports, etc.

The seminar will take place on November 6, 2026, during class. Each group will have 10 minutes for the presentation, followed by 3 minutes of feedback.
Objective

In this seminar, students are expected to propose and critically evaluate a Computer Vision solution using Deep Learning, demonstrating understanding of all pipeline stages—from problem definition to critical analysis of results. Presentations must highlight the reasoning behind architectural choices and the impact of the techniques applied.
2. Requirements

    Selection of an appropriate dataset from Kaggle or a similar platform (e.g., classification, object detection, segmentation, or generative tasks).

    Implementation of a complete solution, including data preprocessing, model construction, and Deep Learning training.
